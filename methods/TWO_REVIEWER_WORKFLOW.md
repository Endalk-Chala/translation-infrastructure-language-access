# Two-Reviewer Workflow

This document records the human-evaluation procedure for the project **When Translation Is Not Access: AI Translation Infrastructure and Communicative Inequality in Under-Resourced Languages**.

## Review design

Two researchers independently evaluate the same blinded translation outputs in Amharic and Afaan Oromo. Reviewers should not know whether Output A or Output B was produced by Google Translate or Meta SeamlessM4T v2 during the independent scoring stage.

Each output is scored on four 1–5 dimensions:

- semantic fidelity
- pragmatic fidelity
- institutional fidelity
- actionability

Reviewers may also assign one or more qualitative error tags and add explanatory notes.

## Procedure

1. Reviewer 1 independently scores all blinded outputs.
2. Reviewer 2 independently scores the same outputs without seeing Reviewer 1's ratings.
3. Original ratings are locked conceptually and preserved as the reliability dataset.
4. Inter-rater agreement is calculated before any discussion of disagreements.
5. Reviewers then compare discrepant cases and discuss the source of disagreement.
6. Adjudicated final scores, error codes, and notes are entered separately.
7. Original Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 ratings are never overwritten.
8. System identity is revealed only after independent scoring and adjudication are complete.

## Why independence matters

The purpose of independent coding is to estimate how consistently the codebook can be applied. Discussing cases before reliability is calculated would artificially inflate agreement and make the reliability estimate less informative.

## Adjudication

Adjudication is a separate analytical stage from reliability. It produces the final interpretive dataset used for system comparison and qualitative analysis.

For each disputed item, reviewers should record:

- the dimension(s) in disagreement
- the initial Reviewer 1 and Reviewer 2 scores
- the reason for disagreement
- the adjudicated score
- any final error tag(s)
- a short adjudication note when the case is theoretically or institutionally important

## Core rule

Do not repair a translation mentally. Reviewers should score the output a target-language reader actually receives, not the meaning the reviewer believes the system intended to produce.
